# Three reference ads, taken apart

All three are TikTok downloads (576×~1024, 30fps, HE-AACv2 audio). Each one is a Zack D Films-style 3D story video with a brand ad added to the end. I watched them as 2 fps contact sheets plus a close-up of the seam, with ffmpeg cut detection and an audio-level profile. The dialogue comes from the burned-in captions. Video 2's part 2 has no captions and couldn't be transcribed here, so its words are unknown.

Across the three:

| | Ending type | Hinge | Seam | Part 2 share | Part 2 length |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Pole vault → BRGR BOX | Momentum | Flight lands on the roof, mid-sentence | Continuous camera, no cut | 39% | 14.6s |
| 2. Gas drip → a location | Zoom-out / shape | Globe → Maps globe dives to a building | Hard cut, globe matches globe | ~20% | ~7.3s |
| 3. Drainpipe → Local Area Burger | Arrival | "came out on the other side" → "to Local Area Burger" | Hard cut, captions identical | 58% | 25.9s |

What all three have in common:
1. Part 1 is a complete, satisfying video on its own terms, so the viewer has already decided to watch.
2. Part 2 starts *on the ending*: the last line or the last image, not something new.
3. The brand lands in the first 1–2s of part 2.
4. Part 2 copies part 1's surface first (caption style, narrator energy, or the shape on screen), then becomes the ad.
5. The ad half is conventional and sensory: fast real shots, one benefit per shot, a plain CTA, and a logo or address end card.

Reference 3 shows that a long part 2 (58%) can still work when the hinge is strong. But it's the one that feels most like an ad by the end. For Vilas we cap part 2 at ~40% and 15s.

## 1. Pole vault over the Empire State Building → BRGR BOX (37.1s)

Part 1 VO, from the captions: "If you try to pole vault over the Empire State Building, you would fly up about 20ft before smashing into it. Now if you dug a giant slot in front and ran faster than a race car while hugging a pole that's almost 2000 feet long and four feet thick, you could plant it into the slot and the pole would snap back sending you flying…"
Part 2: "…BRGR BOX, where you can get the best burgers in all of Birmingham. Mouthwatering mix grills and much more. For the freshest food in town, best flavours, BRGR BOX is definitely worth a visit!"

- 0–22.5s: one continuous follow-cam with zero hard cuts. Red measurement brackets and counters tick up (20 ft, 2,000 ft, 4 ft, 187 ft/sec).
- 22.5s **seam**: the flying character crosses from CG into real drone footage of the restaurant roof and lands by the storefront. The camera never cuts, the sentence never ends, and the audio level stays flat.
- 24–37s: real food shots, hard cuts every ~0.7s from 26.4s, the brand said twice, "worth a visit!", logo card.
- Note: this one was cut *into* part 1's final motion, which our Rule zero forbids. Our version of a momentum hinge starts *after* the last frame and continues its motion (an image-to-video bridge from the last frame).

## 2. Spilled gas at the pump → a location on Maps (37.0s)

Part 1 VO, from the captions: "When you take the nozzle out of your car, a few drops of gasoline spill to the ground. Now it would only be about a few milliliters of gas [2.3 ml counter]. But around 37 million cars fill up every day [in the U.S.] and most spill just a little as the nozzle comes out. If you collected every drop from every gas station in the U.S., you'd have around 37,000 gallons. That's enough to drive a car around the entire planet nearly 30 times." It ends on a spinning globe with "30x" above it.

- ~29.7s **seam**: hard cut from the 3D globe to a screen recording of a maps app showing the same globe, centred, at about the same size. It zooms down through the continent and region to a satellite view of one building (with a pin), and ends on a street-level view of that building, held ~5s.
- Part 2 audio is noticeably louder than part 1 (RMS roughly doubles), with no captions.
- Lesson: a **shape match** (globe → globe) carries the viewer across a hard cut, and the **zoom-in** reverses part 1's zoom-out. Cheap to make: it's just a screen recording. Its weak spot is that the level jump and missing captions make the seam obvious, so match your loudness and captions.

## 3. Pulled into a drainpipe in a flood → Local Area Burger (44.2s)

Part 1 VO, from the captions: "He was pulled into a drainpipe after being caught in a flood. A nearby officer rushed over to help but he was quickly dragged in too. The rushing water forced them both through the 100-foot-long pipe. They finally came out on the other side…"
Part 2: "…to Local Area Burger. We've got a chill, cozy place to grab some amazing juicy burgers that are super easy on your wallet. Forget that greasy drive-thru, get a real fix with our juicy smash burgers. Swing by tonight and treat yourself." The end card gives the street address and "4pm–12am | Closed on Monday".

- 0–18.4s: part 1, night-time flood, mostly one continuous camera.
- 18.4s **seam**: hard cut from the two characters crawling out of the pipe exit to a low, tilted shot of the neon "Local Area Burger" sign. The caption chunk "to Local Area Burger" uses the **exact same caption style** as part 1, so the sentence reads as one.
- 18.4–44s: phone-shot real footage of the food truck, the burgers, the grill, the crowd, the staff. Cuts every ~1–1.5s, and the same caption style throughout.
- Lesson: the **arrival** hinge is the simplest and strongest. Any ending where someone ends up somewhere can be pointed at the brand with one preposition. Matching the caption style does most of the work of making it seamless.

## Worked examples: how I'd do each one for Vilas

- **Drainpipe (arrival):** "They finally came out on the other side…" → "…of vilas.studio." The bridge picture is the round pipe exit match-cut to the round camera cutout of a phone, pulling back to show the phone showing the Vilas site. Turn: "Getting dragged a hundred feet through a pipe is optional. Looking legit online isn't." Offer: "Websites for local businesses that look like they cost ten times more." (over the gallery) / "From $300." (over a demo, labelled demo). Sign-off: "vilas.studio."
- **Gas (big number + zoom-out):** after "…nearly 30 times." → "Or you could drive to the bakery down the street. If you can find it." Maps-style dive into a main street, then a phone on a counter showing the bakery demo (labelled demo). Turn: "37,000 gallons is a lot. A website that looks this good isn't." Offer + sign-off as above. The arithmetic has to be in the plan if you quote any dollar figure for the gas.
- **Pole vault (momentum):** a bridge shot from the last frame. The flyer keeps going, over the skyline and through a café window, and the camera pushes into the laptop on the table, open on vilas.studio. "…straight onto vilas.studio, where a local business gets a site that looks expensive. It wasn't."
